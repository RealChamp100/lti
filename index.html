When responsibility is greater than authority

Some of the most important work inside organisations crosses reporting lines. The person responsible for making progress often depends on people they cannot simply direct.

Cross-functional projects, transformation programmes, employee networks, change initiatives and internal working groups all depend on people choosing to contribute and follow through.

But the person leading the work may have significant responsibility without having formal authority over the people needed to deliver it.

That means they need to do more than communicate well.

They need to create clarity, establish ownership, make decisions, influence stakeholders, build support and keep people moving towards a shared outcome.

Leading Through Influence gives them a practical way to do that.

Who the programme is for

The programme is designed for employees leading work that depends on people outside their direct reporting line. It is particularly relevant for people leading:

  • Cross-functional projects
  • Transformation and change
  • Employee Networks and Employee Resource Groups
  • Communities and working groups
  • Strategic initiatives involving multiple teams or functions

Participants do not need to be formal people managers.

What matters is that they are responsible for making something happen through other people.

What participants learn

The programme helps participants build six core capabilities.

01

Direction and priorities

Clarify the purpose of the work, define what matters most and create a shared understanding of what success looks like.

02

Roles, ownership and delegation

Bring together the right people, clarify responsibilities and delegate meaningful work without becoming responsible for everything themselves.

03

Planning and decision-making

Turn priorities into realistic plans, establish who can make which decisions and prevent everything from becoming dependent on the person leading the initiative.

04

Stakeholders and influence

Identify the people who can enable or block progress, understand what matters to them and build support deliberately.

05

Communication and delivery

Create useful ways of coordinating work, following up on commitments and addressing problems before they become blockers.

06

Collaboration and team effectiveness

Build an environment where people can contribute, challenge ideas, give feedback, handle disagreement and improve how they work together.

A practical system participants can use in their work

The programme is built around the practical questions people need to answer when they are leading through influence.

Where are we going?

Create a clear purpose, define priorities and agree what success looks like.

Who owns what?

Clarify roles, responsibilities and boundaries so that important work has clear ownership.

How will decisions get made?

Establish who can decide what, what needs escalation and how to keep work moving.

What can we realistically deliver?

Connect priorities to time, people, capacity and resources.

Who needs to be involved?

Identify stakeholders, understand competing priorities and build support with the people who matter.

How will we work together?

Create effective communication, useful meetings, clear commitments and accountability.

Frequently asked questions

Is this only for employee networks and Employee Resource Groups?

No.

ERG leaders are a clear example of people who need to lead through influence rather than hierarchy, which is why some of our client work has involved employee networks.

The same skills apply to transformation, cross-functional projects, internal change and other work where someone needs to mobilise people they do not manage.

Do participants need to be people managers?

No.

The programme is specifically useful for people who carry responsibility beyond their formal authority or reporting line.

Can the programme be adapted to our organisation?

Yes.

The core framework stays consistent, but examples, exercises and emphasis can be adapted to the work participants are responsible for and the challenges they are facing.
